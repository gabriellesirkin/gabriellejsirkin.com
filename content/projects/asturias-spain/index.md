description: |-
  Highly recommend your next vacation is getting in a car or van with no destination and no plan for where you’ll sleep.

  Led by curiosity and a quiet desire to see as few people as possible, we followed our instincts. Each morning over coffee, we’d open the maps app and let a random pin decide.

  Some days were misses; bad meals, towns drowning in tourists, 48 hours bathed only by the sea. Camping on a beach one night, we met Yann, a Frenchman who’d fished his own dinner that night. In broken English he said, “Not every day is good. Some days, pfffff. But it’s okay. Good days make it worth it.”

  He was right. On the great days or moments, we were first in the water before the beach filled up, wandered through unmarked villages buying the morning’s first baguette, stumbled into a restaurant packed with drunk locals before 11 am just as the fisherman dropped off the fresh catch, walked an hour lost in the rain at midnight pulled by the most incredible smells of blossoming flowers. It was a reawakening. Novelty mixed with childhood nostalgia, joy and discovery.

  On a long walk along the sea, we spotted graffiti that translated from Spanish, “to define is to limit.” It became my mantra through the rest of the trip, through Liguria with friends and Rome visiting my family. The moment you start planning or reaching for a best-of list, you give up your agency and your time. The novelty you were seeking is already everyone else’s.

  Travel is a privilege which encourages the practice of surrender and gratitude. It breaks routine and pulls you back to something you forgot you missed.

  I have worked in the travel space for a long time. My best advice is; get lost. You will find.
